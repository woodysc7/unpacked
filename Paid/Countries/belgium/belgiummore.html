A Brief History of Belgium

Pre-Roman and Roman Belgium (Before 476 CE)

Belgium’s history traces back to ancient times when Celtic and Germanic tribes inhabited the region. The Romans, expanding their empire, conquered the area known as Belgica in 57 BCE. Roman rule brought urbanization, infrastructure, and Latin influence, shaping the land that would later become Belgium.

Medieval Flanders and Burgundy (476–1482)

Following the fall of the Western Roman Empire, Flanders emerged as a powerful medieval county, known for its prosperous trade and cloth industry. In the 15th century, Burgundy’s dukes united Flanders with their own territories, creating a wealthy and culturally vibrant region at the crossroads of Europe.

Spanish and Austrian Habsburg Rule (1482–1713)

Through marriage, the Low Countries, including present-day Belgium, came under Habsburg rule. This period saw religious strife, including the Dutch Revolt against Spanish rule, leading to the division of the region along religious lines. The Peace of Westphalia in 1648 formally recognized the independence of the Dutch Republic.

Dutch and French Rule (1713–1830)

After the War of Spanish Succession, the Austrian Netherlands (Southern Netherlands) fell to the Austrian Habsburgs, while the Northern Netherlands gained independence as the Dutch Republic. Later, during the French Revolutionary Wars, Belgium was annexed by France. French rule introduced legal reforms and abolished feudal privileges.

Belgian Independence and Growth (1830–1914)

In 1830, Belgium declared independence from the Netherlands after the Belgian Revolution. The country adopted a liberal constitution and quickly industrialized, becoming a major industrial power in Europe. The late 19th century saw Belgium’s colonial expansion into Africa, particularly in the Congo.

World Wars and Modernization (1914–1945)

Belgium’s neutrality was violated in World War I, leading to significant devastation. During World War II, the country was again occupied by Germany. The post-war period ushered in economic recovery, European integration, and the development of a welfare state.

European Union and Multicultural Society (1945–Present)

Belgium played a key role in the formation of the European Union, hosting its headquarters in Brussels. The country evolved into a multicultural society, with regions like Flanders and Wallonia having distinct cultural identities and languages. Despite occasional political tensions, Belgium remains a symbol of peaceful coexistence and European unity.

Languages of Belgium

Languages of Belgium

Belgium is a multilingual country with three official languages: Dutch, French, and German. The linguistic landscape reflects the country's diverse cultural heritage and history of regional autonomy. Each language community has its distinct regions and institutions.

Dutch in Belgium

Dutch is the most widely spoken language in Belgium, primarily in the northern region of Flanders. Known as Flemish in Belgium, Dutch is used in government, education, and daily life in Flanders and the Brussels-Capital Region.

French in Belgium

French is spoken mainly in the southern region of Wallonia and the Brussels-Capital Region. It is the primary language of government and administration in these areas. In Brussels, both French and Dutch are official languages due to its bilingual status.

German in Belgium

German is spoken by a small community in the eastern part of Belgium, particularly in the region of the German-speaking Community. This community has its own government and institutions to preserve and promote the use of the German language.

Belgium Cuisine

Cuisine

Belgium may not be the first country that comes to mind when you think of culinary destinations, but its food scene is a delightful surprise waiting to be explored. Belgian cuisine is a fusion of French and Dutch influences, with a touch of German influence in some regions. Known for its rich and hearty dishes, Belgian food is all about comfort and indulgence, often accompanied by the country's famous beers and chocolates.

  • Moules Frites – Steamed mussels cooked in white wine and served with crispy fries, a Belgian classic.
  • Carbonnade Flamande – Beef stew cooked in beer with onions and mustard, a hearty and flavorful dish.
  • Waterzooi – A creamy fish or chicken stew made with vegetables and herbs, originating from Ghent.
  • Belgian Waffles – Light and crispy waffles topped with a variety of toppings like whipped cream, strawberries, and chocolate.
  • Stoofvlees – A slow-cooked beef and beer stew, a favorite comfort food in Belgium.
  • Frites – Belgian fries are a must-try, often served with a wide range of sauces like mayo, ketchup, andalouse, or samurai.
  • Speculoos – Spiced shortcrust biscuits that are often enjoyed with coffee or used in desserts.
  • Chocolate – Belgium is renowned for its high-quality chocolates, with pralines, truffles, and bars available in a myriad of flavors.
  • Beer – Belgium boasts a rich beer culture with a wide variety of styles including abbey ales, lambics, and saisons.

Food Culture

Belgians take their food seriously, with an emphasis on quality and taste. Meals are often leisurely affairs, enjoyed with friends and family. Dining out is a social event, and Belgians appreciate good food and drink in a relaxed setting. Be prepared to savor each bite and take your time to appreciate the flavors and textures of Belgian cuisine.

Belgium Festivals

What are Belgium’s most famous festivals?

Belgium may be small in size, but it packs a punch when it comes to vibrant and diverse festivals that showcase the country’s rich culture and traditions. Here are some of the most iconic celebrations to experience:

  • Tomorrowland (Boom, July) – Known as one of the world’s largest electronic music festivals, Tomorrowland transforms the town of Boom into a magical wonderland of music, art, and dance.
  • Carnival of Binche (Binche, February) – This UNESCO-recognized event features the famous Gilles, costumed characters who parade through the streets throwing oranges to ward off evil spirits in a centuries-old tradition.
  • Gentse Feesten (Ghent, July) – The Ghent Festival is a lively mix of music, street theater, art exhibitions, and culinary delights that turn the city into a buzzing cultural hub for ten days.
  • Chocolate Festival (Brussels, February) – Indulge your sweet tooth at the Chocolate Festival in Brussels, where you can sample delectable chocolates, attend workshops, and marvel at chocolate sculptures.
  • Ommegang Festival (Brussels, July) – This historical reenactment in Brussels brings the Renaissance era to life with colorful processions, jousting tournaments, and traditional music and dance.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Belgium, be prepared for varying weather conditions, especially in outdoor events. Purchase tickets in advance for popular festivals like Tomorrowland. Respect the local customs and traditions, such as wearing a Gilles costume or indulging in delicious Belgian chocolates. Embrace the festive spirit, interact with locals, and immerse yourself in the unique cultural experiences that each festival offers.
