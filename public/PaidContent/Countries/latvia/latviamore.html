A Brief History of Latvia

Early Settlement and Medieval Period (Before 1561)

Latvia's history dates back to ancient times when Baltic tribes, such as the Latgalians and the Livonians, inhabited the region. In the 12th century, Germanic crusaders, known as the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, arrived, bringing Christianity and establishing control over the area. Riga, founded in 1201, developed as a major trading hub within the Hanseatic League, shaping Latvia's cultural and economic ties with Europe.

Swedish and Russian Rule (1561–1918)

During the Livonian War in the 16th century, Latvia came under Swedish rule, leading to a period of relative stability and prosperity. However, in the early 18th century, Latvia was incorporated into the Russian Empire following the Great Northern War. Russian control brought about a Russification policy, impacting Latvian culture and language.

Independence, Soviet Occupation, and World Wars (1918–1945)

After the collapse of the Russian Empire in 1917, Latvia declared its independence in 1918. The interwar period was marked by nation-building efforts, cultural flourishing, and democratic governance. However, in 1940, Latvia was occupied by the Soviet Union, then by Nazi Germany during World War II, and again by the Soviets in 1944. These occupations brought immense suffering, including deportations, executions, and forced resettlements.

Soviet Era and Independence Restoration (1945–1991)

Under Soviet rule, Latvia experienced political repression, economic hardship, and Russification policies. The late 1980s saw a resurgence of Latvian nationalism, leading to the declaration of independence in 1990. The restoration of Latvia's independence in 1991 marked the end of Soviet domination and the beginning of a new era for the country.

European Integration and Modern Latvia (1991–Present)

Since gaining independence, Latvia has focused on rebuilding its economy, strengthening democratic institutions, and integrating into the European Union and NATO. The country has embraced its cultural heritage, revitalized its language, and promoted economic growth. Today, Latvia is known for its vibrant arts scene, picturesque landscapes, and unique blend of Baltic, Nordic, and European influences.

Languages of Latvia

Languages Spoken in Latvia

Latvia is officially a bilingual country, with Latvian as the official language and Russian recognized as a minority language. Latvian, also known as Lettish, is a Baltic language and holds great cultural significance. While Latvian is the predominant language spoken by the majority of the population, especially in urban areas, Russian is commonly used among the Russian-speaking minority, particularly in Riga and other urban centers.

English Proficiency in Latvia

English proficiency in Latvia is generally good, especially among the younger generation and those working in the tourism industry. Approximately 60-70% of Latvians report having at least some knowledge of English, with a significant portion being conversational or fluent. In major cities and tourist areas, signage and information are often available in English, making it relatively easy for English-speaking travelers to navigate and communicate.

Latvia Cuisine

Cuisine

Latvian cuisine reflects the country's history and geography, with influences from neighboring countries like Russia, Germany, and Sweden. While not as well-known globally as some other European cuisines, Latvia offers hearty and flavorful dishes that highlight local ingredients like grains, potatoes, dairy, and fish. Traditional Latvian food tends to be simple yet satisfying, with a focus on preserving the natural flavors of the ingredients.

  • Rupjmaize – Dense and dark rye bread, a staple in Latvian meals.
  • Grey Peas with Bacon – A classic Latvian dish featuring dried peas cooked with savory bacon.
  • Speķa Pīrāgi – Bacon-filled pastries, popular as a snack or appetizer.
  • Sklandrausis – A sweet pastry made with rye dough and filled with carrot and potato mash.
  • Jāņu Siera Kūka – Midsummer festival cheesecake with a light, creamy texture.
  • Zirņi ar Speķi – Pea soup with smoked bacon, a comforting and flavorful dish.
  • Karbonāde – Breaded pork chops, a beloved comfort food in Latvia.
  • Silķe ar Biezpienu un Sīpolu Mērci – Herring with cottage cheese and onion sauce, a popular seafood dish.
  • Ķimeņu Sēnes – Caraway seed mushrooms, a savory side dish often served with meat.

Mealtimes

In Latvia, mealtimes follow a similar pattern to other European countries. Breakfast is usually light, with options like yogurt, porridge, or bread with cheese. Lunch is the main meal of the day, enjoyed between 1 pm and 3 pm, often consisting of soup, a main course, and dessert. Dinner is typically lighter and eaten around 7 pm to 9 pm.

Latvia Festivals

What are Latvia’s most famous festivals?

Latvia’s rich cultural heritage is showcased through a variety of vibrant festivals that celebrate traditions, music, and art throughout the year. Join the locals in these unique and lively celebrations:

  • Līgo and Jāņi (Nationwide, June 23-24) – Celebrated during the summer solstice, this ancient pagan festival features bonfires, traditional songs, flower crowns, and jumping over bonfires for good luck.
  • Rīga City Festival (Riga, August) – The capital city comes alive with music, dance performances, art exhibitions, and delicious food stalls, showcasing the best of Latvian culture.
  • Carnival in Riga (Riga, February) – A colorful and lively event featuring parades, street performances, and vibrant costumes, adding a festive atmosphere to the winter season.
  • Midsummer Festival (Nationwide, June 23) – A joyous celebration of the longest day of the year, filled with traditional folk songs, dancing, wreath-making, and feasting on seasonal delights.
  • Latvian Song and Dance Festival (Riga, July) – A grand cultural event held every five years, where thousands of singers and dancers from all over Latvia come together to perform traditional songs and dances, showcasing the country’s cultural pride.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Latvia, embrace the local customs and traditions with an open heart. Sample traditional Latvian cuisine, join in the folk dances, and interact with friendly locals to truly immerse yourself in the festive spirit. Don’t forget to check the festival schedules in advance, as some events may require tickets or have specific timing. Pack layers for unpredictable weather, comfortable shoes for walking, and a camera to capture the colorful moments of these unique cultural celebrations.
