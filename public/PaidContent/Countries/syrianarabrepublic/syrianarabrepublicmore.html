A Brief History of Syrian Arab Republic

Ancient Syria and Roman Influence (Before 636 CE)

Syria's history dates back to ancient Mesopotamian and Egyptian civilizations, with cities like Damascus and Aleppo becoming key trade hubs. The region was conquered by the Romans in the 1st century BCE, leading to the incorporation of Syria into the Roman Empire. Roman rule brought urban development, trade networks, and the spread of Christianity which would have a lasting impact on the region's culture.

Byzantine Era and Islamic Conquest (636–1516)

After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, Syria came under the control of the Byzantine Empire. However, in 636, Arab Muslim forces defeated the Byzantines at the Battle of Yarmouk, marking the beginning of Islamic rule in the region. Under the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, Damascus became a thriving center of Islamic culture and learning, shaping the religious and architectural landscape of Syria.

Crusades, Mongol Invasions, and Ottoman Rule (1097–1918)

During the Crusades, Syria became a battleground between Christian crusaders and Muslim forces, leading to a series of conflicts and shifting control of the region. In the 13th century, the Mongols swept through Syria, causing widespread destruction. Eventually, the Ottoman Empire gained control of Syria in the 16th century, ruling the region for over 400 years and leaving a significant mark on Syrian society and administration.

French Mandate and Independence (1918–1946)

Following World War I, Syria fell under French control as part of the League of Nations mandate system. The Syrian people resisted French rule, leading to a protracted struggle for independence. In 1946, Syria finally gained independence, establishing a republic and setting the stage for a tumultuous period of political instability and military coups.

Ba'ath Party Rule and Civil Conflict (1963–present)

The Ba'ath Party seized power in Syria in 1963, ushering in a period of authoritarian rule marked by political repression and human rights abuses. In 1970, Hafez al-Assad took control of the country in a bloodless coup, establishing a dynasty that would rule Syria for decades. His son, Bashar al-Assad, succeeded him in 2000 and faced widespread protests during the Arab Spring in 2011, leading to a brutal civil war that continues to devastate the country.

Languages of Syrian Arab Republic

Languages in Syrian Arab Republic

Syria is a diverse country with Arabic as its official language. However, there are also other languages spoken by various ethnic and religious groups, reflecting the nation's rich cultural tapestry.

English Proficiency in Syria

English is not as widely spoken in Syria as Arabic, but you can find English speakers in major cities and tourist areas. Around 12-15% of the population reports proficiency in English, with higher rates among younger generations and those in the tourism industry.

Useful Arabic Phrases

  • Marhaban – Hello
  • Maa as-salama – Goodbye
  • Min fadlak – Please
  • Shukran – Thank you
  • Hal tatakallam al-ingliziya? – Do you speak English?
  • La atakallam al-arabiya – I don’t speak Arabic
  • Wayn al hammam? – Where is the bathroom?

Syrian Arab Republic Cuisine

Cuisine

Syrian cuisine is a delightful fusion of flavors influenced by Middle Eastern, Mediterranean, and Levantine culinary traditions. The food is rich in spices, herbs, and fresh ingredients, offering a unique and flavorful dining experience. While Syrian cuisine may not be as well-known globally as some other Middle Eastern cuisines, it certainly deserves recognition for its delicious and diverse dishes.

  • Kibbeh – A popular dish made of bulgur, minced meat, and spices, often served fried or baked.
  • Hummus – A creamy dip made from chickpeas, tahini, olive oil, and lemon juice, often garnished with herbs and spices.
  • Shawarma – Thinly sliced meat (usually lamb, chicken, or beef) wrapped in pita bread with vegetables and sauces.
  • Falafel – Deep-fried chickpea patties, crispy on the outside and soft on the inside, served with tahini sauce.
  • Moutabbal – Roasted eggplant dip similar to baba ghanoush, blended with tahini, garlic, lemon juice, and olive oil.
  • Maqluba – A savory upside-down rice dish with layers of meat, vegetables, and nuts, often served at festive occasions.
  • Tabbouleh – A refreshing salad made with bulgur, parsley, mint, tomatoes, onions, and dressed with olive oil and lemon juice.
  • Baklava – Layers of filo pastry filled with nuts and sweetened with honey or syrup, a popular dessert in Syria.
  • Labneh – Thick, strained yogurt often served with olive oil, herbs, and bread as a dip or spread.

Mealtimes

In Syria, mealtimes are a social affair, often enjoyed with family and friends. Lunch is typically the main meal of the day and is served in the early afternoon, followed by a siesta. Dinner is lighter and eaten later in the evening, allowing for a leisurely dining experience

Syrian Arab Republic Festivals

What are Syria’s most famous festivals?

Syria’s rich cultural heritage is celebrated throughout the year with a variety of colorful and traditional festivals that showcase the country’s history and customs. Here are some of the most renowned festivals in Syria:

  • Damascus International Film Festival (Damascus, November) – A prestigious event that brings together filmmakers and cinema enthusiasts from around the world to celebrate the art of filmmaking amidst the historic backdrop of Damascus.
  • Palmyra Festival (Palmyra, May) – Held in the ancient city of Palmyra, this festival celebrates the archaeological wonders of the region with cultural performances, music, and art exhibitions against the stunning backdrop of ancient ruins.
  • Saladin Festival (Latakia, July) – Honoring the legendary Muslim leader Saladin, this festival features historical reenactments, traditional music, and dance performances that commemorate the region’s rich history.
  • Aleppo International Music Festival (Aleppo, September) – A celebration of classical and contemporary music, this festival brings together musicians and orchestras from Syria and beyond for captivating performances in the historic city of Aleppo.
  • Arabesque Festival (Damascus, March) – Showcasing the beauty of traditional Arabic arts and crafts, this festival features vibrant bazaars, art exhibitions, and cultural performances that highlight Syria’s artistic heritage.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Syria, respect local customs and traditions to fully immerse yourself in the cultural experience. Check the festival schedules and book accommodations in advance, especially during peak festival seasons. Be open to trying local cuisine, engaging with locals, and participating in the festivities to make the most of your festival experience in Syria.
