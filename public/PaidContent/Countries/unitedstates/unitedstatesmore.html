A Brief History of United States

Indigenous America

It is well known that the Americas were inhabited by indigenous civilizations prior to European colonization. Indigenous people had a patchwork of civilizations developed in complete isolation from the rest of the world, resulting in some of the most interesting and varied cultures in the totality of history. This variety would face near total annihilation under the boot of European disease and cruelty.

American Revolution (1776–1783)

After Colombus’ ‘discovery’ of the Americas, European powers spent the next centuries dueling for supremacy of the New World. The colonial US was financed by cash crops like sugar and tobacco and built by imported African slaves. By the eighteenth century, the modern day East Coast was held firmly in the hands of British Empire. Amid growing development and nationalism came calls for independence.

Early Republic and Westward Expansion (1783–1860)

The early years of the republic were marked by debates over the balance of power between states and the federal government. Westward expansion, driven by Manifest Destiny, saw the United States acquiring vast territories through treaties, purchases, and conflicts. The nation's economy grew with the rise of industry and the expansion of slavery, leading to increasing tensions between the North and South.

Civil War and Reconstruction (1861–1877)

The issue of slavery culminated in the Civil War, fought between the Union (Northern states) and the Confederacy (Southern states). The Union victory in 1865 led to the abolition of slavery and the reunification of the country. Reconstruction aimed to rebuild the South and grant civil rights to newly freed African Americans, but the period was marred by racial violence and political struggles.

Industrialization and Progressive Era (1877–1917)

The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw rapid industrial growth, urbanization, and immigration. Industrialists amassed wealth, leading to the rise of labor movements and calls for social reforms. The Progressive Era saw efforts to address social issues, regulate big business, and expand democracy, laying the groundwork for modern government policies and social programs.

World Wars and Cold War (1917–1991)

The United States emerged as a global power during and after World War I, promoting democracy and international cooperation. In World War II, the U.S. played a crucial role in defeating the Axis powers. The subsequent Cold War rivalry with the Soviet Union shaped American foreign policy and domestic affairs for decades, influencing areas such as military spending, scientific advancement, and civil rights movements.

Contemporary America (1991–Present)

Since the end of the Cold War, the United States has faced new challenges and opportunities in a rapidly changing world. Economic globalization, technological advancements, and cultural shifts have reshaped American society. Issues such as terrorism, climate change, and social inequality continue to shape national debates and policies, reflecting the ongoing evolution of the United States in the 21st century.

Languages of United States

Languages in the United States

You need English proficiency to get around the United States, although I’d make a case for Spanish as well allowing you to communicate with the large Hispanic population in the States. Locals are not patient with poor or bad English, and expect most visitors to the United States to speak coherent English.

English in the United States

English is the predominant language spoken in the United States, with about 80% of the population over the age of five reporting that they speak only English at home. Overall, around 94% of the U.S. population speaks English proficiently. In major cities and tourist areas, you will find English widely spoken, but in some rural communities or among recent immigrants, Spanish may be more common.

United States Cuisine

Cuisine

Like the rest of the US, American food is heavily stereotyped, mostly as greasy and unhealthy. It is. It is also delicious and maybe the most diverse on the planet. Avoid any place that advertises itself as American food, it will likely be bland and overcooked. Instead, try international Americanized food, it is oftentimes very good and completely distinct from where it came from. When Americans go to a restaraunt they almost always get Americanized international food.

  • Burgers and Fries – An American classic, juicy burgers topped with cheese, lettuce, tomato, and served with crispy fries.
  • BBQ Ribs – Slow-cooked and smothered in tangy barbecue sauce, these ribs are a staple in Southern cuisine.
  • Macaroni and Cheese – Creamy macaroni pasta baked with a cheesy sauce, a beloved comfort food across the country.
  • Clam Chowder – A hearty soup made with clams, potatoes, onions, and cream, popular in New England.
  • Texas Chili – A spicy stew made with minced beef, beans, tomatoes, and chili peppers, served with cornbread or crackers.
  • Apple Pie – A quintessential American dessert, sweet and tart apple filling baked in a flaky crust.
  • Buffalo Wings – Deep-fried chicken wings coated in a spicy and tangy buffalo sauce, typically served with blue cheese dressing and celery sticks.
  • Key Lime Pie – A refreshing dessert made with key lime juice, egg yolks, and sweetened condensed milk in a graham cracker crust.
  • Philly Cheesesteak – A sandwich filled with thinly sliced steak, melted cheese, and onions, a Philadelphia favorite.

Fast Food

Fast Food

Fast food is about as American as it gets. Cheap and delicious, it is an American necessity. The general rule is, the more Southern you are the better the chain is. Yes to Chic-Fil-A, In-N-Out, Dunkin Donuts and Zaxby’s. No to McDonalds, KFC, Burger King and Arby’s.

United States Festivals

What are the United States' most famous festivals?

The United States boasts a diverse tapestry of festivals that reflect the rich cultural heritage and vibrant spirit of its people. From music extravaganzas to quirky celebrations, here are some of the most iconic festivals across the country:

  • Mardi Gras (New Orleans, Louisiana, February/March) – Experience the ultimate party during this flamboyant carnival with colorful parades, masked balls, and lively street performances.
  • Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival (Indio, California, April) – A mecca for music lovers, this epic event features top artists, art installations, and a bohemian atmosphere in the desert.
  • Burning Man (Black Rock City, Nevada, August/September) – Immerse yourself in a temporary city of radical self-expression, art installations, and communal living in the heart of the Nevada desert.
  • Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta (Albuquerque, New Mexico, October) – Marvel at the spectacle of hundreds of colorful hot air balloons painting the sky during this mesmerizing nine-day event.
  • St. Patrick's Day Parade, Boston – While originating in Germany, Oktoberfest celebrations in the U.S. offer a taste of Bavarian culture with beer, music, and traditional food.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in the United States, plan ahead and book accommodations well in advance, especially for popular events. Stay hydrated, wear sunscreen, and be prepared for varying weather conditions depending on the event location. Familiarize yourself with local customs and regulations, and respect the environment by following leave-no-trace principles at outdoor festivals. Embrace the diverse experiences, engage with fellow festival-goers, and savor the unique moments that each celebration offers.
