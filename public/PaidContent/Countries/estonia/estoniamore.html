A Brief History of Estonia

Prehistoric Settlements and Baltic Tribes

Estonia's history dates back to ancient times when the region was inhabited by Finno-Ugric tribes. The area saw the rise and fall of various tribal groups, engaging in trade with neighboring civilizations like the Vikings. These early settlements laid the groundwork for Estonia's cultural heritage and language.

Teutonic Order and Danish Rule (13th–16th Century)

In the 13th century, the Teutonic Order and Danish forces sought control over Estonia, leading to conflicts and power struggles. The region experienced periods of both German and Danish rule, impacting its political landscape and introducing Christianity to the local population.

Swedish Dominion and the Great Northern War (17th–18th Century)

Sweden's influence over Estonia grew in the 17th century, shaping the region's governance and culture. The Great Northern War in the early 18th century saw Estonia become a battleground, eventually leading to Russian control over the territory and the end of Swedish rule.

Russian Empire and National Awakening (19th Century)

Under Russian rule, Estonia experienced a period of Russification policies, which fueled a growing sense of national identity among the Estonian people. The 19th century saw a cultural renaissance and the emergence of Estonian nationalism, setting the stage for future independence movements.

Independence, Soviet Occupation, and World War II (20th Century)

After declaring independence in 1918 following the Russian Revolution, Estonia faced a brief period of freedom until Soviet forces occupied the country in 1940. World War II brought further turmoil as Estonia was successively occupied by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union, leading to significant human suffering and loss.

Soviet Era and Singing Revolution (1944–1991)

The post-war years saw Estonia incorporated into the Soviet Union, enduring a period of repression and forced assimilation. However, in the late 1980s, the peaceful Singing Revolution emerged, characterized by mass demonstrations and cultural events, ultimately leading to Estonia's independence from the Soviet Union in 1991.

European Union and Modern Independence (1991–Present)

Since regaining independence, Estonia has made significant strides in economic development, technology, and governance. Joining the European Union in 2004, Estonia has embraced its identity as a Baltic state with a dynamic digital society, attracting visitors with its rich history and forward-thinking approach.

Languages of Estonia

What languages are spoken in Estonia?

Estonia's official language is Estonian, a Finno-Ugric language closely related to Finnish. Russian is also widely spoken, especially in areas with a significant Russian-speaking population, such as Tallinn and northeastern Estonia. English proficiency is high among younger generations and in urban areas.

English in Estonia

English is commonly spoken in Estonia, particularly in tourist areas, hotels, and restaurants. Many signs and public transportation information are also available in English, making it relatively easy for English-speaking travelers to navigate and communicate. However, learning a few basic Estonian phrases can be appreciated by locals.

Useful Estonian Phrases

  • Tere – Hello
  • Nägemist – Goodbye
  • Palun – Please
  • Aitäh – Thank you
  • Kas sa räägid inglise keelt? – Do you speak English?
  • Ma ei räägi eesti keelt – I don't speak Estonian
  • Kus on WC? – Where is the toilet?
  • Kui palju see maksab? – How much does it cost?
  • Vabandage – Excuse me / Sorry
  • Laud kahele, palun – A table for two, please
  • Arve, palun – The bill, please
  • Kas saaksite mind aidata? – Can you help me?
Estonia Cuisine

Cuisine

Estonian cuisine is a delightful blend of influences from its Nordic neighbors and Eastern European roots. While the food may not be as widely known as some other European cuisines, Estonia offers hearty and comforting dishes that showcase locally sourced ingredients. The cuisine is characterized by simple flavors, often highlighting the natural taste of the main ingredients. Traditional Estonian dishes are perfect for warming up during the cold Baltic winters and celebrating the short but vibrant summers.

  • Kalev's Soup – A hearty soup made with barley, potatoes, meat, and vegetables, named after a legendary Estonian hero.
  • Herring with Sour Cream Sauce – Marinated herring served with a creamy sauce, a popular appetizer in Estonian cuisine.
  • Verivorst – Blood sausage, a traditional Christmas dish in Estonia, usually served with sauerkraut and lingonberry jam.
  • Kama – A unique Estonian dessert made from a mixture of roasted barley, rye, oat, and pea flour, often served with yogurt or kefir.
  • Smoked Fish – Estonia's coastal location means an abundance of smoked fish options, such as smoked salmon and trout.
  • Pirukad – Savory pastries filled with meat, cabbage, mushrooms, or potatoes, perfect for a quick snack or light meal.
  • Russian-inspired Dishes – Due to historical ties with Russia, dishes like borscht (beet soup) and pelmeni (dumplings) can also be found in Estonian cuisine.
  • Marinated Mushrooms – A popular side dish or appetizer, often prepared with dill and onions.
  • Black Bread – A staple in Estonian cuisine, dense and flavorful rye bread served with almost every meal.

Estonia Festivals

What are Estonia’s most famous festivals?

Estonia’s rich cultural heritage comes alive through its diverse and vibrant festivals that offer a glimpse into the country’s traditions and creativity. While there are numerous celebrations throughout the year, here are some of the most renowned ones:

  • Tallinn Music Week (Tallinn, April) – A cutting-edge music festival that showcases a wide range of genres in various venues across the picturesque capital city. Experience the latest sounds from Estonian and international artists.
  • Seto Kingdom Day (Setomaa region, August) – Celebrate the unique Seto culture with traditional music, dance, and cuisine. Witness colorful processions and immerse yourself in the folklore of this distinct ethnic group.
  • Parnu Film Festival (Pärnu, June) – A cinematic celebration attracting filmmakers and enthusiasts from around the world. Enjoy screenings of independent films, discussions, and workshops in the charming coastal town of Pärnu.
  • Joulud Jazz (Tallinn, December) – Jazz up your holiday season with this festive jazz festival held in the heart of Tallinn. Experience the magic of Christmas with soulful music and a cozy atmosphere.
  • Song and Dance Celebration (Tallinn, July) – Experience the power of music and dance at this grand event that brings together thousands of performers in a spectacular display of national pride and unity.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Estonia, consider booking accommodations in advance, especially during peak festival seasons. Embrace the local customs and immerse yourself in the unique traditions of each celebration. Be prepared for varying weather conditions, as Estonian summers can be unpredictable. Don’t miss the opportunity to try local delicacies and interact with friendly locals who are eager to share their culture with visitors.
