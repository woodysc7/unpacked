A Brief History of United Arab Emirates

Pre-Islamic and Early Islamic Period (Before 630 CE)

Before the arrival of Islam, the region now known as the United Arab Emirates was inhabited by nomadic Bedouin tribes who relied on trade, fishing, and agriculture for sustenance. The influence of the Persian and Byzantine Empires, along with trade routes connecting India and the Mediterranean, shaped the cultural landscape. In 630 CE, Islam spread to the Arabian Peninsula, bringing significant changes to the region's social and political structures.

Arabian Peninsula and the Age of Empires (630–1500)

The rise of the Islamic Caliphate brought the United Arab Emirates under the influence of various Islamic dynasties, including the Umayyads, Abbasids, and later the Ottoman Empire. The region's strategic location along trade routes led to the development of vibrant coastal cities like Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Maritime trade flourished, connecting the Emirates to distant lands and cultures.

European Colonialism and the Trucial States (1500–1971)

In the 16th century, European colonial powers, including the Portuguese and British, established trade outposts along the Arabian Gulf. By the 19th century, the coastal Emirates entered into treaties with the British, becoming known as the Trucial States. These agreements provided protection against regional threats and piracy, shaping the Emirates' political landscape.

Formation of the United Arab Emirates (1971–Present)

On December 2, 1971, the seven Emirates—Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al-Quwain, Fujairah, and Ras Al Khaimah—united to form the United Arab Emirates. This union marked a new chapter in the country's history, with Abu Dhabi and Dubai emerging as economic powerhouses fueled by oil wealth and ambitious development projects. The UAE has since diversified its economy, embracing tourism, finance, and technology to drive growth and prosperity.

Languages of United Arab Emirates

Languages Spoken in the United Arab Emirates

The United Arab Emirates (UAE) is a diverse country with Arabic as the official language. However, due to its cosmopolitan nature and large expatriate population, several other languages are commonly spoken, reflecting the multicultural fabric of the nation.

English in the UAE

English is widely used as a second language in the UAE, particularly in urban areas, business settings, and the tourism industry. Around 80-90% of the population in the UAE is proficient in English, making it relatively easy for English-speaking travelers to communicate and navigate the country.

United Arab Emirates Cuisine

Cuisine

The United Arab Emirates offers a diverse culinary landscape that reflects its rich cultural heritage and influences from around the Middle East and beyond. Emirati cuisine is a fusion of traditional Bedouin flavors, Persian, Indian, and Levantine influences, resulting in a vibrant and aromatic dining experience. While the UAE is known for its luxurious dining options, it also boasts a variety of street food and casual eateries where you can savor authentic local flavors.

  • Shawarma – Thinly sliced meat (often chicken or lamb) wrapped in pita bread with garlic sauce, pickles, and vegetables.
  • Mandi – A fragrant rice dish topped with tender, slow-cooked meat, typically served with roasted vegetables and a spicy tomato sauce.
  • Hummus – Creamy chickpea dip seasoned with tahini, olive oil, and spices, served with warm pita bread.
  • Al Harees – A traditional dish made from wheat and meat, slow-cooked to a porridge-like consistency, often enjoyed during Ramadan.
  • Luqaimat – Deep-fried dumplings drizzled with date syrup, a popular sweet treat for special occasions.
  • Arabic Coffee – Strong, cardamom-infused coffee served in small cups as a symbol of hospitality.
  • Al Machboos – Spiced rice dish with meat (usually chicken or lamb), garnished with fried onions and nuts.
  • Al Khameer – Traditional Emirati bread, often served with honey or cheese for breakfast or as a snack.
  • Camel Meat – A delicacy in the UAE, camel meat is lean and has a rich flavor, often prepared in stews or grilled kebabs.

Mealtimes

In the UAE, mealtimes revolve around family and social gatherings. Lunch is usually the main meal of the day, enjoyed by many in restaurants or at home with loved ones. Emirati breakfasts often feature dishes like balaleet

United Arab Emirates Festivals

What are the most famous festivals in the United Arab Emirates?

The United Arab Emirates is a land of rich cultural heritage and vibrant celebrations, with festivals that offer a glimpse into the country's traditions and modern influences. Here are some of the most renowned festivals to experience in the UAE:

  • Dubai Shopping Festival (Dubai, January-February) – A shopper’s paradise, this month-long extravaganza offers unbeatable discounts, live entertainment, fireworks, and raffle draws, making it one of the world's largest shopping festivals.
  • Al Dhafra Festival (Abu Dhabi, December-January) – Celebrating Bedouin heritage, this festival features camel beauty contests, falconry competitions, traditional dance performances, and a bustling souk showcasing local crafts and cuisine.
  • Qasr Al Hosn Festival (Abu Dhabi, February) – Held at the historic Qasr Al Hosn fort, this festival celebrates Emirati culture through art exhibitions, traditional music and dance performances, workshops, and heritage activities for all ages.
  • Ras Al Khaimah Fine Arts Festival (Ras Al Khaimah, February-March) – Showcasing local and international artists, this festival features art exhibitions, workshops, live performances, and cultural events that highlight the growing art scene in the UAE.
  • Sharjah Light Festival (Sharjah, February) – Transforming the city with dazzling light displays, this festival illuminates landmarks, mosques, and historical buildings with artistic light installations, creating a magical nighttime experience for visitors.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in the United Arab Emirates, consider the local customs and dress modestly out of respect for the culture. Stay hydrated, as the desert climate can be intense, especially during outdoor events. Be prepared for large crowds and traffic congestion, so plan your transportation in advance. Embrace the spirit of hospitality and be open to trying traditional foods and participating in cultural activities to fully immerse yourself in the festival experience.
