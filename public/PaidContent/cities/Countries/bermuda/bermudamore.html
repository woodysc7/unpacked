A Brief History of Bermuda

Pre-Settlement Era (Before 1609)

Prior to European arrival, Bermuda was uninhabited, with the island's first known sighting by Spanish explorer Juan de Bermúdez in 1503. However, it wasn't until 1609 that the island would see permanent settlement by English colonists shipwrecked on their way to Virginia. This event marked the beginning of Bermuda's colonial history.

Early Colonial Period (1612–1707)

The Virginia Company established formal governance in Bermuda in 1612, and the island became a critical hub for English colonization efforts in the Americas. With the introduction of cash crops like tobacco and the growth of the maritime industry, Bermuda prospered economically. The island's strategic location also made it a crucial naval base.

Piracy and Privateering (1707–1834)

During the 18th century, Bermuda gained notoriety for its involvement in piracy and privateering activities. Privateers operated with the tacit approval of the British Crown, raiding enemy ships during times of war. This period of maritime lawlessness significantly impacted Bermuda's economy and reputation.

Emancipation and the End of Slavery (1834–1865)

The abolition of slavery in Bermuda in 1834 marked a significant shift in the island's social and economic landscape. Formerly enslaved individuals gained freedom, and the plantation-based economy began to decline. The transition to a free labor system brought about changes in Bermuda's society and culture.

World Wars and Modernization (20th Century)

Bermuda played a strategic role during both World Wars, serving as a military base for Allied forces. The island's economy diversified, focusing on tourism and financial services. In 1968, Bermuda gained internal self-government, followed by the establishment of a new constitution in 1989 that granted further autonomy.

Contemporary Bermuda (21st Century)

In recent years, Bermuda has continued to evolve as a global financial center and popular tourist destination. The island grapples with issues of sustainability, economic diversification, and social equality. Despite its small size, Bermuda maintains a distinct cultural identity shaped by its unique history and blend of influences.

Languages of Bermuda

What languages are spoken in Bermuda?

Bermuda's official language is English, used in government, education, and business. The local Bermudian English dialect may differ slightly from standard British or American English, with unique expressions and pronunciation. As a British Overseas Territory, British English norms prevail.

Regional Dialects and Influences

In addition to English, you may encounter traces of Portuguese and Spanish in Bermudian English due to historical influences from Portuguese and Spanish-speaking sailors. These influences have left their mark on local vocabulary and place names.

Bermuda English Phrases

  • Good day – Hello
  • How de do? – How are you?
  • Tank you – Thank you
  • Ah right – I understand
  • G’bye now – Goodbye
  • Wha gwan? – What’s going on?
  • De plane lef – The plane has left
  • Uh oh, de bus lef me – Oh no, the bus left without me
  • He bin longin’ for a bike – He has wanted a bike for a long time
  • Yuh cahn do dat – You cannot do that
Bermuda Cuisine

Cuisine

Bermudian cuisine offers a delightful blend of flavors influenced by African, Caribbean, Portuguese, and British culinary traditions. While the island's cuisine may not be as widely known as some other Caribbean destinations, the dishes here are a true reflection of Bermuda's unique cultural heritage. Expect a mix of fresh seafood, tropical fruits, and hearty comfort foods that will tantalize your taste buds.

  • Fish Chowder – A rich, tomato-based soup filled with local fish, vegetables, and spices, often served with a splash of rum.
  • Bermuda Fish Sandwich – Fresh fish fillets, typically rockfish or wahoo, served on raisin bread with lettuce, tomato, and tartar sauce.
  • Hoppin' John – A comforting dish made with rice, black-eyed peas, and salted pork, seasoned with herbs and spices.
  • Codfish Breakfast – A traditional Bermudian breakfast dish featuring salted codfish, boiled potatoes, and bananas, often served with a side of avocado and hard-boiled egg.
  • Rum Swizzle – Bermuda's iconic cocktail made with rum, citrus juices, and grenadine, a refreshing drink perfect for hot days.
  • Spiny Lobster – A local delicacy, spiny lobster is often grilled or served in a rich butter sauce, showcasing the island's abundant seafood.
  • Cassava Pie – A savory pie made with cassava, a starchy root vegetable, combined with salted fish, peppers, onions, and seasonings, baked to perfection.
  • Shark Hash – A hearty stew made with shark meat, potatoes, onions, and aromatic herbs, a traditional dish with a unique Bermudian twist.

Local Flavors

Bermuda's cuisine celebrates the island's natural bounty, with an emphasis on fresh, locally sourced ingredients. Bermuda Festivals

What are Bermuda's most famous festivals?

Bermuda may be a small island, but its vibrant culture shines through in its lively festivals that blend local traditions with international influences. Here are some of the most iconic celebrations to experience on this beautiful island:

  • Bermuda Carnival (Hamilton, June) – A colorful explosion of music, dance, and Caribbean vibes takes over the streets of Hamilton during this energetic festival. Join the parade, feel the rhythm, and immerse yourself in the island's joyful spirit.
  • Harbour Nights (Hamilton, summer months) – Running through the summer, this weekly street festival features local vendors, live music, dance performances, and a festive atmosphere along Front Street. Explore the culture, taste the cuisine, and shop for unique souvenirs.
  • Gombey Festival (various locations, various times) – The Gombeys are traditional Bermudian dancers dressed in vibrant costumes who perform to rhythmic drumbeats. Experience their mesmerizing moves and learn about Bermuda's rich heritage at this lively festival.
  • Bermuda Heroes Weekend (various locations, June) – Celebrating the island's cultural heroes, this weekend-long festival includes a mix of events like a J'ouvert celebration, a Parade of Bands showcasing elaborate costumes, and beach parties. Join in the festivities and feel the pride of Bermuda's heritage.
  • Bermuda Festival of the Performing Arts (Hamilton, January-February) – Showcasing a diverse range of artistic talents, this annual festival features performances in music, dance, theater, and more. Immerse yourself in the arts, attend world-class shows, and appreciate the cultural richness of Bermuda.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Bermuda, remember to embrace the island's laid-back vibe and friendly atmosphere. Check the festival schedule in advance and plan your accommodations accordingly, especially during peak tourist seasons. Don't miss the chance to sample local cuisine and interact with Bermudians to truly experience the warmth of the community. Respect local customs, stay hydrated in the island heat, and above all, dance, laugh, and create unforgettable memories during your festival adventures in Bermuda.
