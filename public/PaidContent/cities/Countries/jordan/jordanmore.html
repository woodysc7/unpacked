A Brief History of Jordan

Ancient Jordan (Pre-6th Century BCE)

Jordan's history dates back to ancient times when it was part of various civilizations, including the Ammonites, Edomites, and Moabites. The region's strategic location at the crossroads of important trade routes made it a melting pot of cultures and influences.

Nabatean Kingdom and Roman Rule (6th Century BCE–4th Century CE)

The Nabateans, known for their rock-cut architecture in Petra, thrived as a major trading power in the region. In 63 BCE, Jordan came under Roman control and was incorporated into the province of Arabia Petraea. Roman rule brought infrastructure development and urbanization to the region, leaving a lasting impact on Jordan's landscape.

Byzantine and Islamic Period (4th Century–7th Century)

Jordan became part of the Byzantine Empire in the 4th century, with Christianity spreading across the region. In the 7th century, Islamic armies conquered Jordan, ushering in a new era of Arab-Islamic rule. The Umayyad Caliphate established its capital in Damascus, influencing Jordan's culture and architecture.

Crusader and Mamluk Rule (12th Century–16th Century)

During the Crusades, Jordan saw conflicts between European crusaders and Muslim forces, leading to the establishment of Crusader castles like Kerak and Shobak. The Mamluks, a powerful military caste, later took control of the region, shaping its political landscape until the rise of the Ottoman Empire.

Ottoman Rule and World War I (16th Century–20th Century)

Jordan came under Ottoman control in the 16th century, becoming part of the Ottoman province of Syria. The region played a significant role in World War I as the British and Arab forces, led by T.E. Lawrence, fought against the Ottomans. The war led to the eventual establishment of the Emirate of Transjordan under British mandate.

Independence and Modern Jordan (20th Century–Present)

Jordan gained independence in 1946, with the Hashemite dynasty ruling the newly established kingdom. The country faced challenges, including conflicts with Israel, waves of Palestinian refugees, and regional instability. Despite these challenges, Jordan has emerged as a relatively stable country in the Middle East, known for its hospitality, historical sites, and natural wonders.

Languages of Jordan

What languages are spoken in Jordan?

Jordan's official language is Arabic, specifically the Levantine dialect. English is widely understood, especially in urban areas and tourist destinations. Additionally, some Jordanians speak French, German, or Italian due to historical ties and education.

English Proficiency in Jordan

English is considered the second language in Jordan and is taught in schools from an early age. Approximately 70-75% of Jordanians have some level of English proficiency, with higher rates among the younger population and those working in tourism-related industries.

Jordan Cuisine

Cuisine

Jordanian cuisine offers a delightful blend of flavors influenced by its Middle Eastern and Mediterranean neighbors. Traditional dishes are hearty and rich, often featuring a mix of herbs, spices, and fresh ingredients. Jordanian food can be a true culinary adventure, showcasing a range of textures and tastes that will leave any food enthusiast satisfied.

  • Mansaf – Considered the national dish of Jordan, mansaf is a flavorful combination of lamb cooked in a tangy yogurt sauce, served on a bed of rice and garnished with nuts and parsley.
  • Falafel – Deep-fried balls made from ground chickpeas or fava beans, typically served in pita bread with tahini sauce, vegetables, and pickles.
  • Maklouba – A savory one-pot dish featuring layers of rice, vegetables, and either chicken, lamb, or eggplant, all flipped over when served to create an impressive presentation.
  • Musakhan – Roasted chicken seasoned with sumac and onions, served on flatbread and topped with pine nuts.
  • Tabbouleh – A refreshing salad made with finely chopped parsley, bulgur wheat, tomatoes, onions, mint, and dressed with lemon juice and olive oil.
  • Knafeh – A sweet and cheesy dessert made of shredded phyllo dough, filled with rich cheese and soaked in sugar syrup, often topped with crushed pistachios.
  • Shawarma – Thinly sliced meat (commonly lamb, chicken, or beef) wrapped in pita bread with tahini sauce, pickles, and vegetables.
  • Maglouba – A delightful upside-down rice and vegetable casserole, typically including eggplant, cauliflower, or potatoes.
  • Baklava – Layers of phyllo pastry filled with chopped nuts and sweetened with syrup or honey, a popular dessert in Jordanian cuisine.

Jordan Festivals

What are Jordan’s most famous festivals?

Jordan’s rich cultural heritage is celebrated throughout the year with colorful festivals that showcase the country’s traditions and history. Here are some of the most notable festivals to experience in Jordan:

  • Jerash Festival of Culture and Arts (Jerash, July) – Held in the ancient city of Jerash, this festival features music, dance, theater, and art performances against the backdrop of Roman ruins, creating a magical blend of past and present.
  • Aqaba Traditional Arts Festival (Aqaba, September) – Celebrating Jordan’s diverse heritage, this festival highlights traditional music, dance, handicrafts, and cuisine from various regions of the country, offering visitors a vibrant cultural experience by the Red Sea.
  • Amman Citadel Nights (Amman, Summer) – Taking place at the historic Amman Citadel, this festival includes concerts, film screenings, art exhibitions, and cultural activities that bring together locals and tourists in a lively celebration of Jordanian arts and heritage.
  • Madaba Olive Harvest Festival (Madaba, October) – This festival honors the olive tree, a symbol of peace and prosperity in Jordan, with olive picking, traditional music, dance performances, and culinary events that highlight the importance of olive cultivation in Jordanian culture.
  • Petra by Night (Petra, Every Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday) – While not a traditional festival, this enchanting candlelit experience allows visitors to explore the ancient city of Petra under the stars, accompanied by traditional music and storytelling for a magical evening in this UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Jordan, remember to respect local customs and traditions. Dress modestly, especially when visiting religious sites or participating in cultural events. Be prepared for warm weather and consider carrying a reusable water bottle to stay hydrated. Embrace the hospitality of the Jordanian people, try traditional dishes, and immerse yourself in the vibrant cultural experiences that these festivals offer.
