A Brief History of Sri Lanka

Ancient Sri Lanka (Before 483 BCE)

Sri Lanka's history dates back over 2,500 years, with early references in Indian epics like the Ramayana. The island was home to indigenous tribes before the arrival of Indian influences, notably Buddhism. The first Sinhalese kingdom, founded by King Vijaya, emerged around 483 BCE, setting the stage for a rich civilization that blended native and Indian cultures.

Anuradhapura Kingdom (377 BCE–1017 CE)

The Anuradhapura Kingdom, established in 377 BCE, became a center of Buddhist culture and learning. Monarchs like King Devanampiya Tissa embraced Buddhism, which shaped the island's identity and architectural heritage. The kingdom's intricate irrigation systems and stupas showcased Sri Lanka's advanced civilization to the world.

Polonnaruwa Period (1070–1215)

Polonnaruwa rose to prominence as the royal capital in the 11th century, witnessing a flourishing of arts, literature, and commerce. King Parakramabahu the Great's ambitious projects, like the massive Parakrama Samudra reservoir, highlighted Sri Lanka's engineering prowess and cultural sophistication.

Colonial Era (1505–1948)

European powers, starting with the Portuguese in 1505, vied for control of Sri Lanka due to its strategic location and resources. The Dutch ousted the Portuguese in the 17th century, only to cede control to the British in 1796. Under British colonial rule, Sri Lanka's economy transformed, with the introduction of plantation agriculture and infrastructure.

Independence and Post-Colonial Period (1948–Present)

Sri Lanka gained independence in 1948, transitioning into a new era marked by political challenges. The country faced ethnic tensions between the Sinhalese majority and Tamil minority, leading to a protracted civil war that lasted for decades. In 2009, the conflict finally ended, but the scars remain as Sri Lanka continues to navigate its path towards reconciliation and development.

Languages of Sri Lanka

Languages Spoken in Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka boasts a rich linguistic tapestry reflecting its diverse cultural heritage. The official languages are Sinhala and Tamil, but English also plays a significant role in government, commerce, and tourism.

English Proficiency in Sri Lanka

English proficiency in Sri Lanka is notably high compared to many other countries in the region. Approximately 25-30% of the population speaks English, with a higher concentration in urban areas and among the younger generation.

Sri Lanka Cuisine

Cuisine

Sri Lankan cuisine is a delightful fusion of flavors influenced by its diverse cultural heritage. Expect a burst of spices in every dish, with a perfect balance of sweet, sour, salty, and spicy notes. Rice and a variety of fresh tropical fruits and vegetables are staples in Sri Lankan cooking, making it a paradise for vegetarians and vegans. The cuisine is not only delicious but also reflects the warmth and hospitality of the Sri Lankan people.

  • Hopper – A bowl-shaped pancake made from fermented rice flour and coconut milk, often served with egg or seeni sambol (sweet onion relish).
  • Kottu Roti – A popular street food made of chopped roti (flatbread), vegetables, eggs, and meat or seafood, all stir-fried with a blend of spices.
  • Rice and Curry – The quintessential Sri Lankan meal consisting of rice served with an array of curries, including dhal (lentil curry), fish curry, chicken curry, and various vegetable curries.
  • Lamprais – A Dutch-influenced dish featuring rice boiled in stock and then baked with meat, eggplant, and sambol wrapped in banana leaves.
  • Hoppers – Bowl-shaped pancakes made from fermented rice flour and coconut milk, often served with egg or seeni sambol (sweet onion relish).
  • Kukul Mas Curry – Spicy chicken curry cooked with a blend of aromatic spices and coconut milk.
  • Wambatu Moju – Sweet and sour eggplant pickle that adds a tangy flavor to any meal.
  • Pol Sambol – A spicy coconut relish made with grated coconut, chili flakes, red onions, lime juice, and Maldive fish.
  • Kiribath – A ceremonial dish of milk rice cooked with coconut milk, often served during special occasions and festivals.

Sri Lanka Festivals

What are Sri Lanka’s most famous festivals?

Sri Lanka’s rich cultural tapestry is beautifully showcased through its diverse festivals that blend traditions, spirituality, and colorful celebrations. Experience the country's vibrant spirit at these iconic events:

  • Esala Perahera (Kandy, July/August) – Witness the grand procession of elephants, drummers, dancers, and fire twirlers during this ten-day Buddhist festival honoring the Sacred Tooth Relic of the Buddha. The spectacle is a feast for the senses.
  • Vesak (Nationwide, May) – Celebrated during the full moon in May, Vesak commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and death of Buddha. Lantern-lit streets, pandals (themed displays), and acts of charity create a serene and spiritually uplifting atmosphere.
  • Kandy Esala Perahera (Kandy, July/August) – Regarded as Asia's grandest cultural pageant, this festival features nightly processions with beautifully adorned elephants, dancers, and traditional musicians. It’s a mesmerizing display of Sri Lankan heritage.
  • Sinhala and Tamil New Year (Nationwide, April) – Welcoming the traditional New Year, this joyous festival includes games, rituals, sweet treats, and cultural performances. Join locals in preparing kiribath (milk rice) and enjoying the festive spirit.
  • Navam Perahera (Colombo, February) – This vibrant religious procession at the Gangaramaya Temple showcases traditional dance forms, drummers, and beautifully decorated elephants. It’s a captivating blend of spirituality and cultural heritage.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Sri Lanka, respect local customs and dress modestly, especially at religious events. Plan your accommodation in advance as popular festivals attract large crowds. Embrace the spirit of community by participating in cultural activities and trying traditional foods. Stay hydrated, protect yourself from the sun, and be prepared for vibrant music, lively dances, and unforgettable experiences.
