A Brief History of Mauritius

Early Settlement and Arab Influence (Before 1500 CE)

Mauritius, located in the Indian Ocean, was first discovered by Arab sailors in the 10th century. However, it wasn't until the 16th century that the island was visited by the Portuguese. The Dutch were the first to attempt colonization in the early 17th century, establishing a short-lived settlement. During this time, the island was also known as a haven for pirates due to its strategic location.

French Colonial Era (1715–1810)

In 1715, the French took possession of Mauritius and named it Île de France. Under French rule, the island developed a sugar-based economy and saw the arrival of African slaves. The island prospered economically but changed hands several times between the French and the British during the Napoleonic Wars, finally becoming a British colony in 1810.

British Rule and Abolition of Slavery (1810–1968)

Under British rule, Mauritius experienced significant social and economic changes. Slavery was abolished in 1835, leading to an influx of indentured laborers, mainly from India, to work on the sugar plantations. The diverse cultural landscape of Mauritius began to take shape during this period, with influences from Africa, India, China, and Europe blending together.

Independence and Post-Colonial Development (1968–Present)

Mauritius gained independence from Britain in 1968 and became a sovereign nation within the Commonwealth. The country transitioned into a stable democracy with a booming tourism industry, strong agricultural sector, and growing financial services sector. Despite facing challenges such as environmental sustainability and economic diversification, Mauritius has emerged as a regional economic hub and a melting pot of cultures.

Languages of Mauritius

Languages Spoken in Mauritius

Mauritius is a diverse and multicultural country, resulting in a fascinating linguistic landscape. The official language is English, but the most widely spoken and commonly used language is Mauritian Creole. French is also widely spoken, reflecting the country's colonial history. Additionally, many Mauritians are fluent in other languages such as Bhojpuri, Hindi, Tamil, Urdu, and Chinese dialects due to the diverse ethnic groups present on the island.

English in Mauritius

English is the language of government, business, and education in Mauritius. It is widely spoken in urban areas and tourist destinations, making it relatively easy for travelers to communicate. About 75% of the population is reported to be proficient in English, especially in formal settings. In more rural areas, knowledge of English may be less common, but basic communication should still be possible in most places frequented by tourists.

Mauritius Cuisine

Cuisine

Mauritian cuisine is a delightful fusion of flavors from Creole, French, Indian, and Chinese influences, reflecting the island's diverse cultural heritage. The food is a true reflection of the island's history and multicultural society, resulting in a unique and tantalizing culinary experience. While the dishes may not always be overly complex, they are rich in spices and aromas that will leave a lasting impression on your taste buds.

  • Dholl Puri – Thin, soft flatbread filled with ground yellow split peas, served with bean curry, pickles, and chutneys.
  • Boulettes – Savory dumplings made of fish, chicken, or vegetables, typically served in a flavorful broth.
  • Rougaille – A rich tomato-based sauce with meat or fish, often served with rice or bread.
  • Vindaye – Marinated fish or vegetables in a spicy turmeric and mustard seed sauce, a nod to the island's Indian influences.
  • Sweet Potato and Coconut Cake – A popular dessert made with grated sweet potato, coconut, and sugar, often enjoyed with a cup of tea.
  • Bol Renversé – A hearty dish featuring a meat or seafood stir-fry served over a mound of rice and topped with a fried egg.
  • Gateau Piment – Spiced lentil fritters, a popular street food snack with a crunchy exterior and soft interior.
  • Octopus Curry – Tender octopus cooked in a fragrant curry sauce, a favorite seafood dish on the island.
  • Rum-infused Desserts – Indulge in desserts like rum cake, rum-infused fruit salads, and rum-flavored ice creams, showcasing Mauritius' sugarcane heritage.

Mealtimes

In Mauritius, mealtimes are a relaxed affair, mirroring the laid-back island lifestyle. Breakfast typically consists of tropical fruits, bread with jam, and strong local coffee. Lunch is the main meal of the day, often enjoyed with family and friends

Mauritius Festivals

What are Mauritius’s most famous festivals?

Mauritius, known for its stunning beaches and diverse culture, hosts a variety of vibrant festivals throughout the year that showcase the island's rich heritage and lively spirit. Here are some of the most iconic celebrations to experience:

  • Cavadee (Throughout Mauritius, January/February) – A colorful Tamil festival celebrated with processions, rituals, and devotees piercing their bodies with skewers, carrying offerings to Lord Murugan.
  • Chinese Spring Festival (Port Louis, January/February) – Celebrating the Chinese New Year with dragon dances, fireworks, traditional music, and delicious feasts marking the beginning of a new lunar year.
  • Maha Shivaratri (Grand Bassin, February/March) – Hindus from all over the island converge at Grand Bassin to pay homage to Lord Shiva, fasting, singing, and engaging in rituals to seek blessings.
  • Cavendish International Street Food Festival (Grand Baie, October) – A culinary delight featuring local and international street food vendors, live music, cooking demonstrations, and a vibrant atmosphere celebrating the island's diverse gastronomy.
  • Diwali (Throughout Mauritius, October/November) – The Festival of Lights is celebrated with colorful light displays, traditional sweets, fireworks, and prayers symbolizing the triumph of light over darkness and good over evil.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Mauritius, embrace the cultural diversity and immerse yourself in the local customs. Respect religious practices and dress modestly when required. Plan your visit in advance as accommodations can get booked quickly during peak festival times. Don't miss the opportunity to try traditional foods and participate in the celebrations with an open heart and a spirit of curiosity.
