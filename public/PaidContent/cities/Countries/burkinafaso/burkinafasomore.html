A Brief History of Burkina Faso

Early History and Pre-Colonial Times

Before European colonization, the area now known as Burkina Faso was home to various indigenous ethnic groups such as the Mossi, Bobo, Gurunsi, and Lobi. The Mossi Kingdoms, particularly the largest of them, Ouagadougou, emerged as powerful entities with complex societal structures. Trade routes crisscrossed the region, connecting it to the trans-Saharan trade networks and influencing local cultures.

Colonial Rule and French West Africa (Late 19th Century–1960)

France established control over the region in the late 19th century, incorporating it into French West Africa. Burkina Faso, then known as Upper Volta, became a part of this larger colonial administration. The French exploited the region for resources and labor, imposing their language and culture while disrupting traditional societies. Resistance movements, like the Bobo War of 1915, challenged colonial authority.

Independence and the Birth of Burkina Faso (1960)

On August 5, 1960, Burkina Faso gained independence from France under President Maurice Yaméogo. The country's new name, meaning "Land of Honest Men," reflected a desire for a fresh start and a break from its colonial past. However, political instability marred the early years of independence, leading to a series of coups and changes in leadership.

Thomas Sankara and Revolutionary Changes (1983–1987)

In 1983, Thomas Sankara seized power in a popularly supported coup, becoming the President of Burkina Faso. Sankara initiated a series of radical reforms aimed at empowering the poor, promoting self-sufficiency, and combating corruption. His policies focused on grassroots development, women's rights, and environmental protection, earning him both admiration and criticism.

Political Turmoil and Transition (Late 20th Century)

Following Thomas Sankara's assassination in 1987, Burkina Faso experienced a period of political instability and military coups. Blaise Compaoré, Sankara's former ally, took power and ruled for nearly three decades, balancing repressive measures with economic reforms. Protests and uprisings marked his tenure, culminating in his ousting in 2014 amid popular discontent.

Modern Burkina Faso (2014–Present)

Since 2014, Burkina Faso has navigated challenges such as terrorism, ethnic tensions, and economic disparities. The government has faced pressure to address security concerns while promoting development and social cohesion. Despite these obstacles, Burkina Faso continues to strive for stability, democratic governance, and sustainable growth as it shapes its future.

Languages of Burkina Faso

What languages are spoken in Burkina Faso?

Burkina Faso is a multilingual country with over 60 different languages spoken. However, French is the official language and serves as the primary language of government, business, and education. Indigenous languages are also prevalent, with Mooré (Mossi), Dioula, Fulfulde, and Bissa being some of the most widely spoken regional languages.

How easy is it to get around in French?

French is essential for travelers in Burkina Faso, especially in urban areas and formal settings. English is not as commonly spoken outside of major cities and tourist areas. Learning a few basic French phrases will greatly enhance your travel experience and interactions with locals.

Useful French Phrases

  • Bonjour – Hello
  • Comment ça va? – How are you?
  • Merci – Thank you
  • S'il vous plaît – Please
  • Oui – Yes
  • Non – No
  • Excusez-moi – Excuse me
  • Je ne comprends pas – I don't understand
  • Où est la gare? – Where is the train station?
  • Combien ça coûte? – How much is it?
  • Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît – I would like a coffee, please
Burkina Faso Cuisine

Cuisine

Burkina Faso's cuisine reflects the country's diverse cultural influences, blending traditional African recipes with French, Mossi, and Fulani culinary traditions. The food here may not be as well-known globally as other cuisines, but it offers a unique and flavorful experience for adventurous food lovers. While the dishes are not overly spicy, they are rich in flavors and often center around staple ingredients like millet, sorghum, rice, and yams. Expect hearty stews, grilled meats, and vibrant sauces that showcase the country's agricultural abundance.

  • Riz Gras – A flavorful rice dish cooked in a tomato-based sauce with vegetables and protein like chicken or beef.
  • Poulet Bicyclette – Grilled chicken, often marinated in spices and served with a side of couscous or plantains.
  • Brochettes – Skewered and grilled meat, typically beef or lamb, seasoned with local spices.
  • Fufu de Banane Plantain – Mashed plantains served with a savory sauce, a popular side dish.
  • Pâte de Mil – Millet porridge served with a sauce made from tomatoes, onions, and leafy greens.
  • Haricots Verts Sauce d’Arachide – Green beans in a creamy peanut sauce, a common and delicious vegetarian option.
  • Tô – A thick millet or sorghum-based porridge that serves as a staple food in Burkina Faso.
  • Bissap – A refreshing hibiscus drink, sweetened with sugar and sometimes flavored with ginger or mint.
  • Massa – Fried balls of millet or corn dough, a popular street food snack often served with spicy sauce.

Burkina Faso Festivals

What are Burkina Faso’s most famous festivals?

Burkina Faso is known for its vibrant and diverse festivals that showcase the country's rich cultural heritage and traditions. While numerous celebrations take place throughout the year, a few standout festivals capture the essence of Burkinabe culture:

  • Pan-African Film and Television Festival of Ouagadougou (FESPACO, Ouagadougou, February) – FESPACO is Africa's largest film festival, attracting filmmakers, actors, and cinema enthusiasts from around the continent. It celebrates African cinema with screenings, workshops, and awards ceremonies.
  • Bobo-Dioulasso Cultural Week (Bobo-Dioulasso, March) – This week-long festival showcases the cultural diversity of Burkina Faso through music, dance, traditional ceremonies, and craft exhibitions. Visitors can experience the vibrant rhythms and colorful traditions of the country.
  • International Craft Fair of Ouagadougou (SIAO, Ouagadougou, October) – SIAO is a major event for artisans and craftworkers to exhibit their creations, including textiles, pottery, jewelry, and woodcarvings. It offers a unique opportunity to appreciate Burkina Faso's artistic talents and support local craftsmanship.
  • La Semaine Nationale de la Culture (National Culture Week, Nationwide, Various months) – Celebrated in different regions throughout the year, this cultural festival promotes traditional music, dance, storytelling, and cuisine. It provides a platform for local artists to share their talents and for visitors to immerse themselves in Burkina Faso's cultural mosaic.
  • Dioulasso Stilt Dance Festival (Banfora, November) – This unique festival features dancers performing traditional dances on stilts, showcasing agility, rhythm, and cultural pride. The colorful costumes and energetic performances make it a captivating experience for all attendees.
