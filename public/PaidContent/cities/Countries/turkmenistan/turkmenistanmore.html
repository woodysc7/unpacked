A Brief History of Turkmenistan

Ancient Turkmenistan (Before 4th Century BCE)

Turkmenistan's history traces back to ancient times with nomadic tribes like the Scythians and Parthians roaming the region. These nomads were known for their skilled horseback riding and metalwork, influencing the culture and trade networks of the area.

Parthian and Sassanian Empires (3rd Century BCE–651 CE)

The Parthian and later Sassanian Empires held sway over Turkmenistan, bringing advancements in art, architecture, and trade along the Silk Road. Cities like Nisa flourished as regional centers of power and culture during this period.

Islamic Conquests and Seljuk Rule (7th–13th Century)

In the 7th century, Turkmenistan fell under Islamic rule as Arab armies expanded into the region. The Seljuk Turks later established a presence, influencing Turkmen culture, language, and architecture. This era saw the rise of important cities like Merv and Nisa as centers of Islamic learning and trade.

Mongol Invasions and Timurid Empire (13th–16th Century)

The Mongol invasions of the 13th century brought devastation to Turkmenistan, but out of the chaos emerged the Timurid Empire, led by the famous conqueror Timur (Tamerlane). Timur's rule left a lasting impact on the region's art, architecture, and political landscape.

Ottoman and Persian Influence (16th–19th Century)

During the following centuries, Turkmenistan experienced a blend of Ottoman and Persian cultural influences. The region became a battleground for these empires, leading to shifts in power and trade routes. Cities like Ashgabat and Mary grew as important centers of commerce and culture.

Russian Conquest and Soviet Era (19th–20th Century)

In the 19th century, Turkmenistan came under Russian imperial control, leading to significant changes in governance, infrastructure, and demographics. The region later became part of the Soviet Union, experiencing rapid industrialization, collectivization, and cultural suppression during the Soviet era.

Independence and Modern Turkmenistan (1991–Present)

Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, Turkmenistan declared independence. The country faced challenges in transitioning to a market economy and establishing a democratic system of governance. Today, Turkmenistan is known for its rich cultural heritage, including traditional crafts, music, and architecture, as it continues to navigate its path in the modern world.

Languages of Turkmenistan

Languages in Turkmenistan

Turkmenistan's official language is Turkmen, reflecting the country's cultural heritage and identity. Russian is also widely spoken, particularly in urban areas and among older generations due to historical ties with the Soviet Union. Various minority languages are spoken in specific regions, adding to the linguistic diversity of the country.

English in Turkmenistan

English proficiency in Turkmenistan is limited, with only about 5-10% of the population reported to have some knowledge of English. In major cities and tourist areas, there may be more English speakers, especially among younger people and those working in the tourism industry. However, travelers should not rely solely on English for communication outside these areas.

Turkmenistan Cuisine

Cuisine

Turkmen cuisine reflects the country's nomadic heritage and diverse cultural influences, offering a unique blend of flavors and ingredients. While not as widely known as other cuisines in the region, Turkmen dishes boast a rich history and a focus on locally sourced ingredients. Expect hearty and filling meals that often center around meat, dairy, and grains, with a variety of spices adding depth to the flavors.

  • Plov – A traditional rice dish cooked with lamb, carrots, and spices, often served at celebrations and gatherings.
  • Manty – Steamed dumplings filled with minced meat and onions, a popular street food and comfort dish.
  • Shashlik – Skewered and grilled meat, usually lamb or beef, marinated in spices and served with flatbread.
  • Samsa – Flaky pastries filled with minced meat, onions, and sometimes pumpkin or potatoes, perfect for a quick snack.
  • Ashlyamfu – A cold noodle soup made with vinegar, vegetables, and sometimes meat, ideal for hot summer days.
  • Chalpamak – A savory porridge-like dish made with wheat, meat, and broth, providing warmth and sustenance during colder months.
  • Yaprak Dolma – Grape leaves stuffed with a flavorful mixture of rice, meat, and herbs, a labor-intensive but delicious dish served on special occasions.
  • Gözleme – Thin, savory flatbreads filled with various ingredients like meat, cheese, or herbs, cooked on a griddle.
  • Sorpa – A nourishing soup made with meat, vegetables, and grains, often enjoyed as a comforting meal.

Turkmenistan Festivals

What are Turkmenistan’s most famous festivals?

Turkmenistan may be a lesser-known destination, but its festivals offer a glimpse into the rich cultural heritage and traditions of this Central Asian country. Experience the warmth and hospitality of the Turkmen people through these unique celebrations:

  • Nowruz (Nationwide, March 21st) – Nowruz, the Persian New Year, is a significant cultural event celebrated by Turkmen communities with traditional music, dance, feasting, and the iconic horse races known as “Galkynysh.” It symbolizes renewal and the arrival of spring.
  • Melons Festival (Mary, August) – Mary, known as the “Melon Capital” of Turkmenistan, hosts a lively festival dedicated to the country’s favorite fruit – the juicy and sweet melon. Enjoy tastings, contests, music, and cultural performances amidst the vibrant atmosphere.
  • Equestrian Games (Ashgabat, September) – Turkmenistan’s passion for horses is showcased in the annual Equestrian Games, featuring traditional Turkmen horseback riding, skill demonstrations, and competitions. Witness the beauty and grace of these magnificent animals.
  • Carpet Festival (Ashgabat, October) – The art of carpet-weaving is central to Turkmen culture, and the Carpet Festival celebrates this craftsmanship with colorful displays, exhibitions, demonstrations, and sales of intricately woven carpets. Immerse yourself in the beauty of traditional Turkmen textiles.
  • Independence Day Celebrations (Nationwide, October 27th) – Turkmenistan’s Independence Day is a patriotic holiday marked by grand parades, fireworks, concerts, and cultural events across the country. Join the locals in honoring the nation’s history and achievements.

Festival Tips

When attending festivals in Turkmenistan, embrace the local customs and traditions with respect and an open mind. Try traditional Turkmen dishes and delicacies offered during the celebrations. It’s advisable to dress modestly and conservatively, especially in religious or formal settings. Be prepared for warm hospitality, traditional music, and the chance to participate in age-old customs.
